A new chapter at the Bureau

I have joined the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics as Member (Economic Statistics). For years my work has circled the same questions: how prices are measured, how industrial output is counted, and how official numbers can earn public trust. Now I get to work on them from the inside, alongside the people who produce Pakistan's official statistics.

My portfolio covers economic statistics and, more recently, social statistics as well. My priorities are data literacy, both within the Bureau and among the people who use its numbers, and bringing machine learning and new data sources responsibly into the production of official statistics. Read the announcement.

What I work on

Six threads run through my research, teaching and public writing.

Measuring the economy

Price indices, GDP base-year revisions and industrial statistics. My 2008 paper on biases in Pakistan's CPI was one of the first systematic critiques of how we measure prices.

Official statistics for the data age

Machine learning in official statistics, multi-source and administrative data, and a national data infrastructure that connects surveys, registers and new digital sources.

Applied econometrics

Causal inference, graph-theoretic causality, time series and forecasting, published in Taylor & Francis, Springer, SAGE and Elsevier journals.

AI and data governance

How public institutions can use AI responsibly, and why Pakistan's AI ambitions depend first on good data rather than hype.

Data skills for development

R, Quarto and reproducible research for economists, civil servants and students, with more than 60 open repositories of teaching material.

Human development

Food security, nutrition, health and education spending, studied through Pakistan's household surveys.
